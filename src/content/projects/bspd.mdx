## What it is

Formula SAE cars have to carry a Brake System Plausibility Device. It watches the throttle and brake pedal sensors, and if the two say something that shouldn't happen together (full-ish throttle while braking hard), it cuts the car's shutdown circuit. It also has to catch a pedal sensor that has come unplugged.

This is the one I did for SCR26. It's all op-amps, RC timers and diodes, no microcontroller, so there is no code to crash. Five LEDs on the edge of the board (Throttle, Brake, B-Disc, T-Disc, Output) show which condition tripped.

The second path catches a disconnected sensor. Both pedal sensors are ratiometric, 0.5 V to 4.5 V, and each input has a 100k pull-up to 5 V. If a sensor comes unplugged, the line floats up past 4.5 V and U1C or U1D goes high. Those two are diode-ORed into a 10k and 10 uF RC (100 ms), and U3C trips once it passes 3.2 V. The schematic notes tie this to rule IC.4.8.3 (b/c).

## The board

Everything comes in and goes out through one 12-pin connector: 12 V and ground, throttle and brake in, throttle and brake back out to the ECU, and the two sides of the shutdown loop. A small regulator (Q1) makes 5 V for the logic. The three MCP6004 quad op-amps sit down the middle, with the timing caps and diodes next to the stage that uses them, and the test points and trimpots along the right edge where you can get a probe on them.
