## What it is

Formula SAE cars have to carry a Brake System Plausibility Device, and the electronic throttle control (ETC) rules spell out what it has to do. It watches the throttle and brake pedal sensors, and if the two say something that shouldn't happen together (full-ish throttle while braking hard), it cuts the car's shutdown circuit. It also has to catch a pedal sensor that has come unplugged.

<Figure src="img/racing/track.webp" ratio="1340 / 800" frame="print" tilt={-1} caption="USC Racing's car on track. The BSPD sits between the pedal sensors and the car's shutdown circuit." alt="USC Racing's Formula SAE car, number 109, driving past a traffic cone" />

This is SCR26's, and the circuit and the board are my design. It's all op-amps, RC timers and diodes, no microcontroller, so there is no code to crash. Five LEDs on the edge of the board (Throttle, Brake, B-Disc, T-Disc, Output) show which condition tripped.

## How the logic works

Click through the blocks. Every threshold here comes straight from the schematic notes. Most of the design effort went into where these thresholds sit: set too loose and the car doesn't shut down when it should, too tight and noise causes a false trip that kills the car mid-run.

The second path catches a disconnected sensor. Both pedal sensors are ratiometric, 0.5 V to 4.5 V, and each input has a 100k pull-up to 5 V. If a sensor comes unplugged, the line floats up past 4.5 V and U1C or U1D goes high. Those two are diode-ORed into a 10k and 10 uF RC (100 ms), and U3C trips once it passes 3.2 V. The schematic notes tie this to rule IC.4.8.3 (b/c).

## The board

Everything comes in and goes out through one 12-pin connector: 12 V and ground, throttle and brake in, throttle and brake back out to the ECU, and the two sides of the shutdown loop. A small regulator (Q1) makes 5 V for the logic. The four MCP6004 quad op-amps sit down the middle, with the timing caps and diodes next to the stage that uses them, and the test points and trimpots along the right edge where you can get a probe on them.

## Where it ended up

I took it through DRC and generated the fabrication files. Because of team changes the board was never installed or brought up, and no testing or inspection was done, so there are no bench results here.
