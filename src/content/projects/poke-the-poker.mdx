## What it is

Christopher Kim and I built this for EE 250. Players make their move out loud, and a sound sensor on the Raspberry Pi picks it up. How fast you commit becomes your confidence score for that turn. Staying silent past the timeout counts as a fold. At the end of the game the Pi prints a table and a matrix, and a plotting script I run by hand afterward turns them into per-player graphs of confidence and influence. It's meant for commentators and analysts, not the players.

The confidence score is just linear in reaction time, with $T = 10$ s as the timeout:

$$
\text{confidence} = \frac{T - t}{T} \times 100\%, \qquad t \le T
$$

Influence is the more interesting number. Every time someone acts without folding they become the aggressor. For each later decision by somebody else, that counts as a chance for the aggressor to push them out, and a fold counts as a hit. A player's influence per round is hits over chances. If player 1 has already folded and player 4 raises, then player 2 folding gets pinned on player 4, not player 1.

## What didn't work well

- **The sound sensor can't tell a player from the room.** We cut false triggers by raising the threshold, but a mic with frequency filtering would be the real fix.
- **The sensor connector kept cutting out,** which gave us partial or inconsistent readings. We ended up replacing the connectors more than once. Soldered joints or better jumpers would have saved a lot of time.
- **One LED doesn't say much.** It only tells people to wait. A multi-color LED or a small display would show whose turn it is.

We were upfront in the write-up about using ChatGPT: getting Mosquitto running on the Windows laptop, researching scoring models, and debugging the scoring logic and the output matrix.
