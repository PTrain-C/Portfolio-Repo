## What it is

John and I built a cigar-box electric guitar and hid an active notch filter inside it. The filter's job is to take out one note, the open G string at 196 Hz, while the D and B strings on either side still come through. It's a twin-T band-stop with positive feedback added, which is what lets the notch be narrow instead of swallowing half the neck.

## The math

We started from the passive twin-T and wrote KCL at the three nodes: $V_1$ between the two $R$'s and the $2C$, $V_2$ between the two $C$'s and the $R/2$, and the output. Solving those together gives

$$
\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{s^2 + \frac{1}{(RC)^2}}{s^2 + s\,\frac{4}{RC} + \frac{1}{(RC)^2}}
$$

Matching that to the standard notch form $K\frac{s^2 + \omega_0^2}{s^2 + s\,\omega_0/Q + \omega_0^2}$ gives $\omega_0 = 1/RC$, $K = 1$, and $Q = 1/4$. A $Q$ of a quarter is a very wide notch. On its own it would cut the D and B strings almost as much as the G.

The fix is to stop tying the bottom of the T to ground and drive it from a fraction of the output instead, $V_x = \alpha V_{out}$ with $\alpha < 1$. Redoing the same three KCL equations with that one change gives

$$
\frac{V_{out}}{V_{in}} = \frac{s^2 + \frac{1}{(RC)^2}}{s^2 + s\,\frac{4(1-\alpha)}{RC} + \frac{1}{(RC)^2}}
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
Q = \frac{1}{4(1-\alpha)}
$$

The constant term didn't change, so $\omega_0$ stays at $1/RC$ and the notch doesn't move. Only $Q$ goes up, by a factor of $1/(1-\alpha)$.

We designed for $Q = 8.5$, which means feeding back almost all of the output:

$$
\alpha = 1 - \frac{1}{4Q} = 1 - \frac{1}{34} \approx 0.97
$$

## Building it

Component values had to come from a restricted parts list, so we checked each pick against the hand calculations and the LTSpice model before anything went on the board. John did the schematic and board layout in KiCAD and the hot-plate soldering. I hand-soldered the rest, and wired the blue box: the 9 V battery, the filter board, and the jacks, all sitting in the cigar box around the neck.

If I'd checked each connection as I soldered it, I would have caught that in a minute instead of chasing it on the bench.

## Tuning on the bench

In simulation the design gave more than 20 dB of attenuation at the notch. On the bench I recorded measurements on the oscilloscope and a DMM. On the real board, some of the lower frequencies weren't being cut as much as they should have been. The potentiometers were slightly off the target values, so we nudged them up until the response lined up with the design. After that the filter and gain stages did what they were supposed to.

## Before this: a band-pass lab

Earlier in the same class we built a Rauch active band-pass filter on an LM741, aimed at $f_0 = 1$ kHz, $Q = 5$, and $|K| = 2$ with 10 nF caps. I built the breadboard circuit and logged the sweep; John did most of the data analysis. The measured Q came in 8% low, which we put down to part tolerances, since Q in that topology is sensitive to the feedback resistor. That's a big part of why we planned on bench tuning for the notch from the start.
