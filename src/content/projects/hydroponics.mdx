## What it is

A hydroponic system for the Food For Thought Foundation's Farms for Thought program. An Arduino UNO R4 reads pH and electrical conductivity (EC, which tracks how much nutrient is in the water), and when either drifts out of range it doses small amounts from four peristaltic pumps until it's back. It also runs the grow lights on a schedule, keeps the water and air pumps on, and reads a float switch.

## The control loop

Every 10 seconds it runs one dosing cycle. EC goes first on purpose: adding nutrients moves the pH, so if pH were corrected first the EC step would just knock it back out. Locking in EC first means the pH loop isn't chasing a moving target.

Small pulses with a mixing wait between them are the whole strategy: dose a little, let it spread, measure, repeat. That keeps it from overshooting and then dosing back the other way. The pulse caps stop one bad reading from emptying a bottle.

## The bus

Every sensor and pump is an Atlas Scientific EZO board. In the version of the firmware shown here, all seven sat on one I2C bus:

| Device | Address | Job |
| --- | --- | --- |
| pH sensor | `0x63` | Reads pH, 815 ms per read |
| EC sensor | `0x64` | Reads EC, TDS, salinity, and specific gravity as CSV, 570 ms per read |
| Temperature | `0x66` | In the build, not in the control loop yet |
| pH down pump | `0x67` | Doses acid |
| pH up pump | `0x68` | Doses base |
| EC up pump | `0x69` | Doses nutrient concentrate |
| EC down pump | `0x6A` | Doses water to dilute. Set to 0 to disable |

A couple of EZO quirks that cost time and are written into the README now: the pumps only accept a lowercase `d,<ml>` dose command over I2C (uppercase comes back as an error), and volumes can have at most two decimal places, so `d,0.20` works and `d,0.200` doesn't.

## Moving the pumps to UART

Seven devices on one bus worked until it didn't. The four pump drivers started throwing NAK errors: the master sends a pump's address and nothing acknowledges it. I watched it two ways, in the serial monitor from the Arduino's side and with a logic analyzer on SDA and SCL to see what was really on the wire. In the end I migrated the pump communication off I2C and onto UART.

The sensor side had its own bug along the way. Readings were occasionally garbage, with no pattern. The driver loops read bytes until the EZO's end-of-data byte, `0x00`, but once a device has nothing left to send, the I2C bus itself hands back `0xFF`, and the loop was copying those into the buffer. ## The PCB

The circuit was first built and validated on a breadboard. Someone else on the team designed a custom PCB for it, and my job there was to check the PCB's netlist against the breadboard build and confirm the two were electrically the same. The board was not fabricated or brought up beyond that comparison.

I also documented the sensor calibration procedures and the wiring specifications, so the setup can be recalibrated and rewired without me there.
