USC Racing builds a new car for Formula SAE every year, and SCR26 is the one I worked on. The rules require a Brake System Plausibility Device: a standalone circuit that cuts power if the throttle and hard braking show up together, or if a pedal sensor fails. We worked out that board's schematic together on the electrical team, and I did the PCB layout myself.

The board is fully analog. The pedal signals are buffered so the BSPD and the ECU both see them without either loading the other, then compared against trimpot set points. An RC timer means a brief overlap won't trip it, but holding throttle through hard braking for about a second will, and once it trips it latches. A separate 100 ms path catches a sensor that's come unplugged. The full walkthrough, with the schematic broken into sections, is on the project page.

The board is 46.7 by 52.8 mm, two layers, 44 nets. I took it through DRC and produced the fabrication files. It wasn't installed or brought up on the car.

## Harness records

Outside the BSPD, I kept the records for more than 200 wire and cable harnesses across 9 subsystems. Wires follow MIL-STD-681F color coding, connectors follow one naming convention, and the records are version-controlled, so a change to a harness gets written down instead of living in someone's head.
