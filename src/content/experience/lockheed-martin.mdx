I spent the summer as a systems engineering intern on the Aegis Combat System. Most of my work was checking that what the system does matches what it's specified to do, and it's where I learned how requirements drive verification.

## Specified versus measured

Radar data gets turned into kinematics (where a track is and how it's moving), and those have to line up with predicted trajectories and meet timing requirements. I scripted that comparison in MATLAB so it runs the same way every time.

Validating datasets for Aegis shipboard packages in MATLAB and Excel also turned up places where the documented configuration didn't match what was measured. Those went back into the requirements, which I updated in IBM DOORS/Jazz, including changes nobody had written down. I used Confluence on my host team.

## The intern project

At the first meeting of our 13-intern project team, nobody spoke and nobody wanted to start a roadmap. I took the unofficial project manager role, even though I would rather have stayed on my host team work. We defined requirements and SWAP-C (size, weight, power, and cost) for a 1U data center server rack, working from COTS reference hardware, including thermal and power budgets.

I ran weekly scrums, cut the work into check-in sized pieces, took the tasks nobody wanted, presented to Lockheed leadership, and wrote knowledge transfer documents for future teams. The team got a perfect project score.
