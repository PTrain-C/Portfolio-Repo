I spent the summer as a systems engineering intern on the Aegis Combat System, the combat system that ties together radar, weapons, and command on Navy ships. Most of my work was checking that what the system actually does matches what it's specified to do.

## Specified versus measured

The core of it was test data validation. Radar data gets turned into kinematics (where a track is and how it's moving), and those have to line up with the predicted trajectories and meet the timing requirements. I scripted that comparison in MATLAB, so it runs the same way every time instead of being checked by hand.

The same idea applied to configuration. Validating datasets for Aegis shipboard packages in MATLAB and Excel turned up places where the documented configuration didn't match what was measured. Those went back into the requirements: I updated and version-controlled them in IBM DOORS/Jazz, including changes that had never been written down.

## The intern project

I also led a team of 13 interns defining the requirements and SWAP-C for a 1U data center server rack, and we presented the results to Lockheed leadership. 1U is one rack unit, 1.75 inches tall, so everything has to fit in a very thin box, and every one of the four letters pulls against the others.
