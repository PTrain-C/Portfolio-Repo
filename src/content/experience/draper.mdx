I took this semester off from classes on purpose. A normal school schedule wasn't going to give me hands-on hardware time, and Draper would. I'm on the Microelectronics Integration & Test team in the Space Systems business area, and I work on two things.

## ASIC design validation

This one came in October and it's the work I most want to be doing. Before a linear regulator (an LDO) gets integrated into the larger board, it gets tested on its own. I run design validation testing on it, looking at how the output responds to line transients and load transients, using Source Measure Units to drive and measure.

## Flight hardware acceptance testing

The other half is acceptance testing on about 5 batches of 6 identical modules, around 30 units, for a NASA Class B space program. The hardware design and the test plan were done before I got here. I help run it, and it's the same routine every batch.

Because the routine is the same each time, a result that looks wrong stands out. During one vibration test the script aborted. I plotted the data in MATLAB and showed that the cause was a bad sensor reading, not a bad unit, and presented that to the review board.

## Class B, briefly

NASA sorts missions into risk classes A through D under NPR 8705.4. Class B is one step below A: high priority work with a low tolerance for risk, which shows up as tighter requirements on parts, workmanship, handling, and testing than a Class C or D mission would carry.
