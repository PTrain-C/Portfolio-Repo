I'm on the Microelectronics Integration & Test team at Draper, working on flight hardware for a Space Systems program built to NASA Class B risk classification. The hardware, assembled PCBs and microcontroller boards, comes in in production batches. My job is overseeing their acceptance testing.

## What acceptance testing is for

Acceptance tests aren't about proving a design works; that was qualification. They're about proving that this particular unit, from this particular batch, was built right and will survive the ride. Every unit goes through them, which is why it's batch work.

## Class B, briefly

NASA sorts missions into risk classes A through D under NPR 8705.4. Class B is one step below A: high priority work with a low tolerance for risk, which shows up as tighter requirements on parts, workmanship, handling, and testing than a Class C or D mission would carry.
