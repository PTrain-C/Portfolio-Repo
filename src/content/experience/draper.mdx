I'm in the Space Systems business area at Draper, working on hardware built to NASA Class B risk classification. The hardware arrives already assembled. My job is overseeing acceptance testing across multiple unit batches, which includes vibration testing and thermal cycling.

## What acceptance testing is for

Acceptance tests aren't about proving a design works; that was qualification. They're about proving that this particular unit, from this particular batch, was built right and will survive the ride. Every unit goes through them, which is why it's batch work.

## Class B, briefly

NASA sorts missions into risk classes A through D under NPR 8705.4. Class B is one step below A: high priority work with a low tolerance for risk, which shows up as tighter requirements on parts, workmanship, and testing than a Class C or D mission would carry.
